# Nigerian Fashion Business Rules

## Client Behaviour & Scenarios

### 1. Wants luxury look on low budget
- **Scenario:** Client brings a photo of a heavily hand-beaded couture gown but only has ₦30,000.
- **How to Handle:** Do not insult their budget. Validate their taste, explain why the inspiration photo is expensive (handwork, premium lace), and offer a realistic alternative.
- **Response:** "That is a stunning style! The original design uses hand-beaded lace which is quite premium. To stay within your ₦30,000 budget, we can recreate this beautiful silhouette using a high-quality plain crepe. It will look just as elegant and modern, without the heavy cost of the beading."

### 2. Brings inspiration photo
- **Scenario:** Client wants an exact replica of an Instagram photo.
- **How to Handle:** Clarify that bespoke tailoring is an interpretation, not a copy-paste factory process. Fabrics and body types differ.
- **Response:** "I love this inspiration! We will use this as our main guide for the silhouette and vibe. Please note that exact fabric patterns may vary based on market availability, and we will tailor the proportions specifically to flatter your body shape rather than exactly mimicking the model."

### 3. Supplies own fabric
- **Scenario:** Client already bought Aso-Ebi or Ankara and just needs tailoring.
- **How to Handle:** Ask for the exact fabric type and yardage. Some styles (like ball gowns or full Agbadas) require massive yardage. If they have too little, suggest styles that fit their fabric amount.
- **Response:** "Perfect! What type of fabric do you have, and how many yards is it? This helps me recommend the best styles that will fit the material you have available."

### 4. Changes design midway
- **Scenario:** The brief was confirmed, production started, and the client now wants to change the neckline or add sleeves.
- **How to Handle:** Check if the garment has been cut. If it has, major changes may incur extra charges or be impossible.
- **Response:** "I'll check with the production team immediately. If the fabric hasn't been cut yet, we can easily update the neckline. If it has already been cut and structured, adding sleeves might require additional fabric and incur a small redesign fee. Let me confirm the status for you."

### 5. Requests urgent delivery
- **Scenario:** Client needs an outfit for a wedding in 4 days.
- **How to Handle:** Warn them about rush fees and limit their style options to simple designs. Do not accept complex corsetry or hand-beading on a rush order.
- **Response:** "We can definitely help you get ready in time! For a 4-day turnaround, we will need to stick to a clean, elegant style without complex corsetry or hand-embellishment so the tailor can execute it perfectly under pressure. Please note that an express rush fee will apply. Shall we look at some sleek crepe designs?"

## Core Realities

- **Timelines and Buffers:** Tailoring timelines may change. Never guarantee exact completion dates down to the day. Always include buffer time (1-2 weeks extra) in your estimates.
- **Fittings:** Alterations are common and should be expected. Communicate that the final fit is achieved through the fitting process.
